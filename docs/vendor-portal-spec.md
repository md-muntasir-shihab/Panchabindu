# Vendor portal status and requirements

Existing `/vendor` uses role-checked server sessions and derives vendor identity from the account, not request-provided tenant IDs. Vendor workspace and mutation endpoints support products, stock, price proposals, payouts, and profile/onboarding requests, with cross-vendor and CSRF tests. Admin may configure capabilities. New profiles start `pending_onboarding` and must be explicitly activated after all fifteen onboarding answers are present and a review reason is recorded; vendor-submitted answers require approval first. Pending accounts can send onboarding/profile requests but cannot conduct operational transactions. This review is not document/KYC verification.

This is not the complete requested portal. Private image-document upload and per-document super-admin approval/rejection are implemented; see `docs/runbooks/vendor-kyc.md` for limits and verification status. Formal identity/KYC verification policy, document expiry, retention/deletion, detailed contracts, formal disputes, notifications, reconciled statements, comprehensive security/session management and all requested deep links require implementation and end-to-end verification. Payout and financial operations require production-safe, audited controls and real integrations; do not simulate settlement. Customer PII, other vendor data and platform margins must never enter vendor DTOs. Use four existing internal roles and existing runtime documents; never accept vendor identity from browser input.

### Resolved returns (local implementation)
The vendor workspace exposes a read-only, session-vendor-scoped list of resolved returns, with order reference, fault category, quantities, vendor debit and date. Internal platform loss, courier claim, proof and notes are not returned. This is not a return submission, dispute, evidence exchange or support-ticket workflow. The isolated security suite tests tenant filtering and field omission; live data and browser/responsive behavior have not been verified. Formal dispute handling and a relational migration remain incomplete; existing ledger and payout views are not a substitute for a full statement workflow.

### Ledger CSV export (local implementation)
Authenticated vendors can download their own ledger entries for an inclusive UTC date range from the earnings tab. The endpoint queries the complete runtime ledger, not the workspace's 200-entry preview; it rejects ranges over 5,000 entries instead of silently truncating. CSV amounts are integer poisha, text cells are quoted and protected against spreadsheet formulas, and responses are private/no-store. Entries include transfers between escrow buckets and must not be summed as revenue. A separate on-screen bucket roll-forward shows opening, credits, debits and closing derived from all recorded earlier entries, plus payouts recorded paid in the period. It does not claim that the ledger is complete or reconciled with a bank, nor does it supply a tax document or independent payment receipt. The isolated route test covers authentication, tenant filtering, date boundary, injection and row limit; browser and real Postgres remain untested.

### General support tickets (local implementation)
Vendors can create and reply to their own general support tickets; a super-admin can view, reply and close them in the vendor control request inbox. Ticket ownership comes from the signed session; request bodies cannot select a vendor. Pending, suspended and closed vendor profiles can use support as long as their user session remains valid and their profile exists. Writes require CSRF, use per-process rate limits and record actor/action/ticket ID in the audit log without message text. There are at most 20 open tickets per vendor and 100 messages per ticket; closed tickets cannot be reopened. Messages are plain text with no attachments. Support is not a formal dispute process and does not alter financial data. Notifications, pagination, retention/deletion policy and shared rate limiting are not implemented. Stored in the existing phase-4B document, not relational tables. Isolated route tests cover auth, cross-vendor isolation, CSRF, reply/close and audit; live Postgres, conflict behavior and browser/accessibility are not verified.

### Statement scope and caveats (local implementation)
The summary uses signed vendor identity, an inclusive UTC date range, exact integer poisha and un-clamped bucket balances (opening plus signed period activity equals closing). It fails closed on malformed or duplicate vendor ledger records, unsafe aggregates or malformed paid payout records. The payout list shows administrative paid status, requested amount, masked account and transaction reference; it is not proof that a transfer occurred, nor a calculation of fees or net proceeds. The displayed CSV remains ledger rows only, not the summary or payout receipts. The source ledger can be incomplete or inconsistent and is not independently reconciled with orders, the general ledger or a payment provider. Notably, the existing `computeVendorEscrowBalance` payout-hold calculation consumes a zero-amount `PAYOUT_DISBURSED` entry and may not clear its reported hold; this statement deliberately does not present that field as a reconciled balance. The ledger report does not correct historical records. Isolated tests cover arithmetic, UTC boundaries, negative balances, overflow, malformed history, authentication and tenant field isolation. Browser and live Postgres have not been tested.
